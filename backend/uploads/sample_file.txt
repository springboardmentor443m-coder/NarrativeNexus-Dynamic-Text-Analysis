<p>Artificial intelligence (AI) is revolutionizing digital forensics by enabling deeper analysis of <b>system fingerprints</b> and behavioral patterns. 
Researchers now use AI to trace the origin of document scans, leveraging unique noise signatures produced by different scanners. 
However, scraped web data often contains encoding issues like “Â”, “Ã”, or “€” that must be cleaned before model training.</p>

<p>The ScannerTrace project, developed at Cyber Labs, introduces a new approach to scanner identification. 
Each scanner leaves a distinct noise pattern, similar to a digital fingerprint. 
By combining convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with statistical features such as LBP (Local Binary Pattern) 
and FFT (Fast Fourier Transform) energy distributions, the system can pinpoint the source of a scanned document.</p>

<p>Data cleaning is crucial. Techniques include lowercasing, removing special characters, 
and replacing symbols with proper equivalents. For instance, “ThÃ© quick brøwn fôx” 
should become “The quick brown fox”. After normalization, tokenization splits text into words for processing.</p>

<h3>Preprocessing in NLP Pipelines</h3>
<p>Preprocessing involves multiple steps — removing noise, applying stemming or lemmatization, 
and dropping stopwords (“the”, “is”, “in”). Once cleaned, topic modeling techniques like 
Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) can uncover hidden themes in the data.</p>

<p>For example, applying LDA to a corpus of forensic articles may yield topics such as:
1. Scanner classification
2. Document forgery detection
3. Deep learning in evidence analysis
4. Texture and frequency-based features</p>

<div>During early experiments, LDA showed promise in grouping similar documents together, 
though the quality of topics depended heavily on preprocessing.</div>

<h3>Dataset Construction</h3>
<p>To train AI models for forensic scanner identification, researchers compiled 
thousands of authentic and tampered document images. PDFs were converted into high-resolution 
PNGs, and images were categorized as “Original”, “Tampered”, or “Flatfield” (reference scans).</p>

<p>However, in some scraped text, unexpected fragments appeared like:
“console.log('init')”, “var pageTitle = 'ScannerTrace';”, or duplicate paragraphs.
This happens when JavaScript code is included during scraping.</p>

  <p>The CNN model achieved 97% accuracy on authentic data but only 88% on tampered copies.</p>

<p>Notice the repetition — this must be handled during data cleaning by removing duplicates.</p>

<h3>Hybrid Model Architecture</h3>
<p>The proposed hybrid model combines a CNN feature extractor with a handcrafted 27-dimensional 
feature vector. These handcrafted features capture scanner-specific traits such as:
- Sensor noise distribution  
- LBP texture variance  
- Frequency-domain FFT radial energy patterns</p>

<p>By concatenating CNN-based embeddings with statistical descriptors, 
the model learns both spatial and frequency-based information, improving accuracy.</p>

<h4>Applications Beyond Forensics</h4>
<p>Such preprocessing and model fusion methods have applications in other domains — 
medical imaging, document verification, and even art forgery detection. 
Each field faces similar challenges with unstructured or noisy data.</p>

<div class="note">
<b>Key takeaway:</b> Always preprocess before analysis. No model, no matter how advanced, 
can compensate for inconsistent, poorly formatted data. Proper normalization, 
stemming, and stopword removal are essential before LDA or classification.</div>
